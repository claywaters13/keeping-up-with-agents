# go-to-market for ai products

**Maturity: CONTESTED** — Contested — active, unresolved disagreement across talks

*Supporting concept* &middot; discussed across **8** talk(s) by **9** speaker(s)

**Definition:** Selling and distributing AI products — positioning, ICP, sales motion, pricing models, and developer-led distribution.

*Also referred to as: go-to-market motion, founder-led sales, ideal customer profile, land and expand, product positioning, developer tools go-to-market, outcome-based selling, usage-based pricing*

## State of Practice

The conference's dominant GTM answer for AI products is not a sales playbook but a delivery model: forward deployed engineering, now treated as the default motion because agentic platforms are infinitely customizable and enterprise buyers cannot articulate what they'd configure. Speakers converge on the economics — Palantir's ~$4M ACV versus $1.2M for ServiceNow is the proof point, and Sierra argues the endgame is outcome-based pricing, which is only underwritable if you have engineers accountable for the outcome. But the field is openly split on what FDE *is*: Kepler and Decagon insist it's product discovery that must never be run as a GTM extension, while Anthropic's ex-Palantir/Rippling view and Sierra treat it as the commercial engine itself. Everyone agrees on the failure mode — an FDE team with no shared platform underneath is a dev shop whose maintenance costs eat the P&L — and on qualification: FDE only pays when a technical product meets a non-technical buyer with high customization needs. On the self-serve/devtools side the diagnosis is different and simpler: building is cheap, so distribution and pitch legibility are the constraint, with founder brand and a 20-second wound/fix/how framing carrying more weight than product quality.

## Consensus

### An FDE function must build on a platform of shared primitives and generalize anything reusable; per-customer builds from scratch are a business-model failure, not a delivery choice.

## Disagreements

### Is forward deployed engineering a go-to-market function or a product function?

| Position A | Position B |
|---|---|
| FDE is a product strategy and an extension of the product team; treating FDEs as a GTM/sales extension is a mistake only a company with Palantir's balance sheet can afford, and the role should sit at the same bar and reporting structure as product engineering.<br>*[How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Kepler](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-kepler.md), [How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Decagon](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-decagon.md)* | FDE is the commercial motion: it is how you sell a technical product to a non-technical buyer, how you scale design partnerships into enterprise ACV, and — since outcome-based pricing requires someone who can guarantee the outcome — it is what makes the pricing model sellable at all.<br>*[Forward Deployed Engineering 101](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-101.md), [The Dirty Secret of Forward Deployed Engineering](../talks/the-dirty-secret-of-forward-deployed-engineering.md)* |

*Why it matters: It determines whether the team reports into product or revenue, whether it is measured on generalized product output or on bookings and expansion, and whether headcount scales with the roadmap or with the pipeline.*

### Should the forward deployed team own customer adoption, enablement, and change management?

| Position A | Position B |
|---|---|
| No — making customers successful is solutions-architect and system-integrator work; running 101/201 sessions, workshops, and rollout enablement wastes 10x engineers and drives them to quit because they were sold a role they didn't get.<br>*[Forward Deployed Engineering at Cursor](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-at-cursor.md), [How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Kepler](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-kepler.md)* | Yes — the customer is buying an outcome rather than software or hours, every forward deployed engineer is accountable to the customer, and leaving product success to the customer's own implementation ability blocks upmarket and horizontal expansion.<br>*[Forward Deployed Engineering 101](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-101.md), [The Dirty Secret of Forward Deployed Engineering](../talks/the-dirty-secret-of-forward-deployed-engineering.md)* |

*Why it matters: It sets what you can contractually promise (outcome vs. deployment), whether partners or your own payroll absorb rollout cost, and whether the engagement ends at 'it works' or at 'they use it.'*

### Where should a company under resource constraints invest to win a market — narrative and distribution, or embedded delivery?

| Position A | Position B |
|---|---|
| In distribution and founder-led narrative: distribute before the product is ready, invest in the founder's personal brand as the moat, and treat pitch legibility as the thing that decides which products get funded and bought.<br>*[GTM Is You](../talks/gtm-is-you.md), [Your AI Product Will Fail Unless You Can Explain It](../talks/your-ai-product-will-fail-unless-you-can-explain-it.md)* | In embedded engineering delivery: be physically on site because the highest-value insight cannot be surveyed for, win trust by shipping small fixes, and build lock-in by becoming the enterprise's linguistic and ontological foundation.<br>*[How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Kepler](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-kepler.md), [Forward Deployed Engineering 101](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-101.md)* |

*Why it matters: The two allocate the same scarce founder and engineering hours in opposite directions — one toward audience and top-of-funnel signal, the other toward a handful of deep accounts — and they imply different first ten hires.*

## Practical Guidance

**Do:**

- Qualify FDE against the two-axis test: technical product sold to a non-technical buyer (Bai), and high customization against a customer that lacks the internal engineering maturity to self-serve (Brunet).
- Architect every customer-specific solution for the next four customers before they have asked for it (Decagon).
- Upstream every task an engineer has to do manually into the product; Decagon built a self-serve integration path after roughly the 25th hand-built custom integration.
- Get the customer's definition of success — metrics and channels — agreed in writing during the earliest deal conversations.
- Scope engagements as a named problem with directional phases ('phase one and two, these agents, six weeks'), not as an allocation of N engineers for M months.
- Demand a named counterpart working team on the customer side before starting; ask 'who are the people we're going to work with' as a qualification question.
- Open a pitch with the customer's wound — the exact moment they want to throw the laptop out the window — then wound, fix, and how inside about 20 seconds, and pass the 17-year-old comprehension test.
- Replace abstract benefit claims with a concrete before/after: '30 minutes digging through docs and tickets' versus 'one question, answer in 10 seconds with sources attached.'
- Ship anything solvable in under a day of work directly instead of routing it through PM process and product vision; whoever defines the problem owns the solution.
- Staff industry experts consistently across customers in a vertical, and use the industry's vocabulary — the wrong lingo costs credibility immediately.
- Put at least two engineers on any customer engagement so the account is not a single point of failure.
- Tell the customer when your product is the wrong tool for their use case; it builds credibility and generates later opportunities.
- For self-serve devtools, diagnose with product signal versus revenue: signal ahead of revenue means invest in distribution, revenue ahead of signal means invest in product.

**Avoid:**

- Standing up an FDE function without a platform underneath — the maintenance cost of from-scratch builds will eat the P&L before the engineers quit.
- Shipping an agent as an accumulation of customer-specific prompts and patches; it becomes a black box too brittle for the vendor and the customer alike.
- Using the FDE team for product 101/201 sessions, company-wide training workshops, bug write-ups, or documentation updates.
- Accepting 'we need FDEs because we're understaffed' as an engagement rationale — that is staff augmentation, not an FDE project.
- Taking customer requirements documents at face value; customers describe solutions rather than problems.
- Calling anything 'just temporary' — assume every hack ships for 18 months and you support it.
- Opening with category jargon like 'agentic AI orchestration platform' or 'agent observability platform,' or with abstract benefit lines like 'we increase productivity.'
- Early-stage companies using FDEs as go-to-market extensions — that is a move available to Palantir after 20 years and unlimited money.
- Assuming a great product will be found on merit the way it might have been 15 years ago.
- Publishing AI-generated skills, docs, or content without regard for their structure — the result reads as purple-gradient slop rather than something polished.

## Notable Outliers

- Palantir has roughly $4M ACV — the highest of any public SaaS company in the Fortune 500 — against ServiceNow at $1.2M and Workday at ~$600K, with no other public SaaS company cracking half a million. ([Forward Deployed Engineering 101](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-101.md), [6:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KwhgfwOSToQ&t=414s))
- Most software pricing in this market will move to outcome-based, with usage-based pricing as an intermediate step — and forward deployed engineering is the only way to guarantee the outcome you are pricing against. ([The Dirty Secret of Forward Deployed Engineering](../talks/the-dirty-secret-of-forward-deployed-engineering.md), [14:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=880s))
- Enterprises adopt your language, not just your product; becoming the linguistic foundation of the organization is the real lock-in. ([How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Kepler](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-kepler.md), [15:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OMHGsUZiqA&t=920s))
- Staying in San Francisco after YC means fundraising twice as much, twice as fast — and SF billboards work as a traction signal for bootstrapped companies with no funding milestones to announce. ([GTM Is You](../talks/gtm-is-you.md), [4:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=276s))
- Now that AI coding is good, the scarce engineering skill is restraint — not building the fast one-off solution. ([How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Decagon](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-decagon.md), [7:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=458s))
- 2027 will be the year of the 'content engineer,' with declarative content pipelines generating walkthroughs, docs, screenshots, and product updates from the codebase as source of truth. ([Content Is Code](../talks/content-is-code.md), [9:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=574s))
