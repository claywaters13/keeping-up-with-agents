# The Dirty Secret of Forward Deployed Engineering

**Speakers:** [Natalie Meurer](../speakers/natalie-meurer.md)

**Org:** Sierra

**Track:** Forward Deployed Engineering &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Day/Room:** Day 2 — Session Day 1 &middot; Track 8 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Duration:** 16m 49s

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE)

## Summary

Natalie Meurer, head of agent engineering at Sierra and a former Palantir FDE (2016–2021), argues that 'forward deployed engineering' has no coherent definition — it has meant so many different things over time that the term means nothing, even as it becomes the hottest job in AI. She traces the role's evolution at Palantir across distinct 'vintages': platform stability/DevOps around 2008, data integration around 2012, custom solutions (Slate dashboards) in 2016, and customer enablement by 2020 — noting these eras stacked rather than replaced each other. The one constant across every vintage is direct accountability to the customer. Her forward-looking claim is that as coding agents make code cheap to produce and pricing shifts from seat-based to outcome-based, the lines between product, agent, AI, solutions, and customer engineering are blurring, and everything is trending toward forward deployed engineering. Worth watching for the historical framing of the FDE role and the 'what vintage of FDE are you?' interview heuristic.

## Key Points

- Forward deployed engineering lacks a coherent definition — the term describes so many different jobs that it has stopped meaning anything, yet it is currently the hottest job in AI.
- The role began at Palantir as something closer to DevOps, where platform stability constituted the bulk of the job, including 2 a.m. pages about unplugged on-prem instances.
- The 'forward deployed' name originally emphasized physical location — job postings led with where you would sit, because so much of Palantir's ecosystem was on-prem deployments.
- Around 2012 the job added data integration, since data integration software without integrated data is 'a movie theater that's playing nothing'; FDEs modeled customer data into Palantir's ontology.
- By 2016 the role added custom solutions (typically Slate drag-and-drop dashboards), and by 2020 it added customer enablement so Palantir wouldn't have to ship people to Finland or Canberra.
- These phases were not discrete eras — the total FDE jobs-to-be-done accumulated over time, which is why Palantir is such an effective training ground for generalist founders.
- Meurer proposes asking candidates 'what vintage of FDE are you?' as an interview heuristic, since the label alone doesn't specify which set of skills a person actually has.
- As coding agents make code cheap, FDEs can build end-to-end solutions rather than just prototype, while product engineers become more client-facing — the two roles are converging.
- Pricing is shifting along two axes (attributability of outcome and agent autonomy) from seat-based to usage-based to outcome-based, and guaranteeing those outcomes is fundamentally forward deployed engineering work.

## Positions

- The term 'forward deployed engineer' has no coherent definition because it has been used to describe too many different jobs. ([2:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=141s), confidence: stated)
- Forward deployed engineering began as DevOps work, not as customer-facing solutioning; platform stability was the bulk of the early job. ([4:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=244s), confidence: stated)
- The 'forward deployed' label originally referred to physical location because Palantir's deployments were largely on-prem. ([3:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=211s), confidence: stated)
- Data integration software is not useful without someone doing the integration work, which is what made FDEs commercially necessary. ([5:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=328s), confidence: stated)
- A dashboard that doesn't write back to its data source decays in usefulness over time. ([7:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=479s), confidence: stated)
- The FDE eras were not discrete; the total jobs-to-be-done accumulated, making the role the best training ground for generalists. ([9:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=552s), confidence: stated)
- Palantir alumni's success at founding companies is caused by the FDE role training them across many facets of building and selling software. ([9:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=552s), confidence: stated)
- Hiring managers should ask candidates what 'vintage' of FDE they are rather than treating the title as a single skill set. ([9:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=598s), confidence: stated)
- Because coding agents make code cheap, FDEs can now ship end-to-end solutions rather than only prototypes, making them more impactful in product development. ([12:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=754s), confidence: stated)
- Product engineering and forward deployed engineering are converging: product engineers are becoming more client-facing while FDEs contribute to product. ([13:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=801s), confidence: stated)
- Most software pricing in this market will move to outcome-based pricing, with usage-based pricing (e.g. foundation model APIs) as an intermediate point. ([14:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=880s), confidence: stated)
- Guaranteeing customer outcomes is the essential function of forward deployed engineering, which is what makes outcome-based pricing viable. ([14:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=880s), confidence: stated)
- Agent engineering is a subset or flavor of forward deployed engineering focused on outcomes, rather than a fully separate discipline as the speaker argued in 2024. ([15:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=918s), confidence: stated)
- All engineering disciplines that touch customers are trending toward forward deployed engineering. ([15:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=918s), confidence: stated)
