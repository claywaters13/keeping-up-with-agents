# Imagination Engineering: "Live in the future and then build what's missing."

*Program title: Imagination Engineering*

**Speakers:** [Eve Bouffard](../speakers/eve-bouffard.md)

**Org:** Y Combinator

**Track:** Design Engineering &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Day/Room:** Day 3 — Session Day 2 &middot; Track 6 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Duration:** 16m 04s

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z2Erdirpudo)

## Summary

Eve, who leads design at Y Combinator, argues that as models become capable enough to one-shot nearly any build, the binding constraint shifts from engineering execution to imagination — coming up with ideas worth building. The talk is mostly a live demo of a week-long experiment she calls 'thinking in public': she brain-dumped her stream of consciousness into a YC Slack channel called Eve Thoughts, then had Claude Opus 4.8 aggregate it into a personal website (evebufar.com) with a design system, a voice.md distilled from her public writing, and custom skills. She also shows 'Shape of Minds,' a tool built that same morning that compares history's most prolific thinkers across dimensions like obsessions, routines, and quirks. The through-line is Paul Graham's 'live in the future and then build what's missing' applied as software-on-demand: whatever stream of consciousness you have, hand it to an agent. It's a short, demo-heavy, inspirational talk rather than a technical one — watch it for the 'thinking in public' framing and personal-tooling patterns, not for implementation depth.

## Key Points

- The speaker's core thesis is that as models get good enough to one-shot almost anything, the bottleneck moves from building to inventing ideas that don't yet seem possible.
- She extends 'building in public' to 'thinking in public': maintaining a Slack channel of raw stream-of-consciousness thoughts that trusted colleagues can read, inspired by a colleague at Conductor who did the same.
- A week of unstructured Slack brain-dumps was enough context for Opus 4.8 to generate a coherent personal website that inferred her interests in art, her reading list, her tools, and her favorite quotes.
- She generates persistent context artifacts for agents — a voice.md distilled from her tweets and public writing, a design-language file that auto-updates when components change, and a glossary .md that agents read instead of parsing the rendered page.
- Feeding agents a compact markdown glossary is described as substantially more efficient than having the agent read the page itself.
- 'Shape of Minds,' built in a single morning session, profiles historical thinkers across dimensions (thinking, obsessions, quirks, origins, routines, output) and surfaces cross-cutting commonalities — many were childless, napped, and ate little.
- She predicts the future of design will be largely sliders — parameterized, user-customizable surfaces rather than fixed layouts.
- The practical habit she recommends is software-on-demand and report-on-demand: any passing curiosity or annoyance (a Slack emoji picker, Renaissance patronage) should be handed to an agent immediately.

## Positions

- The scarce resource in AI engineering is shifting from implementation ability to idea generation, because models will soon one-shot almost any build. ([0:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z2Erdirpudo&t=1s), confidence: stated)
- Sharing your raw thinking process publicly ('thinking in public') is as valuable a practice as building in public, and Paul Graham's essays are the proof case. ([3:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z2Erdirpudo&t=187s), confidence: stated)
- A week of unstructured Slack stream-of-consciousness is sufficient context for a model to build a coherent, personalized website about you. ([2:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z2Erdirpudo&t=137s), confidence: implied)
- Giving an agent a hand-maintained markdown glossary of a project's content is more efficient than having the agent read the rendered page. ([11:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z2Erdirpudo&t=689s), confidence: stated)
- The future of design interfaces is dominated by sliders and user-adjustable parameters rather than fixed designed states. ([6:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z2Erdirpudo&t=374s), confidence: stated)
- Current models are a qualitative step beyond the 'bicycle for the mind' framing — a rocket ship rather than a bicycle. ([5:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z2Erdirpudo&t=301s), confidence: stated)
- Any recurring personal friction (like finding a Slack emoji) is now worth building a custom tool for rather than tolerating. ([12:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z2Erdirpudo&t=779s), confidence: implied)
- Personal learning should be delegated to agents generating short custom reports consumable during commutes rather than through long-form self-study. ([14:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z2Erdirpudo&t=852s), confidence: stated)
