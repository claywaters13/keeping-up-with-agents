# go-to-market for ai products

**Maturity: CONTESTED** — Contested — active, unresolved disagreement across talks

*Supporting concept* &middot; discussed across **9** talk(s) by **11** speaker(s)

**Definition:** Selling and distributing AI products — positioning, ICP, sales motion, pricing models, and developer-led distribution.

*Also referred to as: go-to-market motion, founder-led sales, ideal customer profile, land and expand, product positioning, developer tools go-to-market, outcome-based selling, usage-based pricing*

## State of Practice

The dominant GTM debate at this conference was not pricing or channel but labor: who stands between an agentic product and a customer who cannot implement it themselves. Six of the nine talks are about forward deployed engineering, and the field has converged on a shared diagnosis — because agentic platforms are inherently customizable, most vendors now find themselves selling a highly technical product to a buyer who cannot articulate what it does, which breaks classic self-serve and SLG motions. The consensus prescription is a senior, customer-facing engineering function that builds only on top of shared platform primitives, generalizes every bespoke solution toward the next four customers, scopes engagements around a named problem with written success criteria, and staffs by industry vertical for vocabulary credibility. What remains genuinely unsettled is org placement: Kepler argues FDE is a product-discovery strategy that early-stage companies must never spend as a sales extension, while Palantir- and Sierra-lineage speakers treat customer accountability and outcome guarantees as the whole point — Sierra goes further and predicts most software pricing moves to outcome-based, which only works if someone owns the outcome. Outside the FDE track, the same 'building is cheap, reaching people is not' logic shows up as distribution advice: founder personal brand as moat, pitching the customer's wound before the architecture, and content pipelines generated from the codebase.

## Consensus

## Disagreements

### Is forward deployed engineering a go-to-market motion or a product-discovery function that must be kept out of sales?

| Position A | Position B |
|---|---|
| FDE is fundamentally a commercial motion: it is how you sell a technical product to a non-technical buyer, it is a design partnership scaled into enterprise, and its output is customer accountability and guaranteed outcomes that justify high ACV and outcome-based pricing.<br>*[Forward Deployed Engineering 101](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-101.md), [The Dirty Secret of Forward Deployed Engineering](../talks/the-dirty-secret-of-forward-deployed-engineering.md), [Forward Deployed Engineering at Cursor](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-at-cursor.md)* | FDE is a product strategy — the mechanism by which you discover what to build. Palantir's GTM framing was applied after the fact, and an early-stage company that spends FDEs as sales extensions is burning its scarcest engineers on work that produces no product leverage.<br>*[How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Kepler](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-kepler.md), [How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Decagon](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-decagon.md)* |

*Why it matters: It determines who the function reports to, whether its success metric is bookings or generalized product capability, and whether a 30-person startup should build one at all.*

### Should forward deployed engineering be a distinct organization with its own hiring pipeline and specialization, or the same role as product engineering?

| Position A | Position B |
|---|---|
| Keep it one role. FDE and product engineering share a bar, a reporting structure, and often the same team; the lines are blurring in both directions as coding agents let customer-facing engineers ship end-to-end and product engineers go client-facing.<br>*[How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Decagon](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-decagon.md), [The Dirty Secret of Forward Deployed Engineering](../talks/the-dirty-secret-of-forward-deployed-engineering.md)* | Run it as a deliberate, separately-scoped function with its own hiring profile and its own boundaries on what work it will and will not accept — and first prove you actually need one rather than building it because it is in vogue.<br>*[Forward Deployed Engineering at Cursor](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-at-cursor.md), [Forward Deployed Engineering 101](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-101.md)* |

*Why it matters: A merged org optimizes for upstreaming and retention but risks starving customer work; a separate org gets focus but must constantly defend against becoming staff augmentation or a training-and-workshops team.*

### Does the customer-facing engineering team own customer success and adoption, or only the extraction of generalizable product insight?

| Position A | Position B |
|---|---|
| Owning the outcome is the job. Leaving implementation to the customer caps your ability to sell upmarket, success means the customer keeps the system running after you leave, and guaranteeing outcomes is what makes outcome-based pricing viable.<br>*[Forward Deployed Engineering 101](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-101.md), [Forward Deployed Engineering at Cursor](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-at-cursor.md), [The Dirty Secret of Forward Deployed Engineering](../talks/the-dirty-secret-of-forward-deployed-engineering.md)* | Making customers successful belongs to solutions architects; an engineer who defines the job as customer success is doing product management plus customer support and failing to steer the product from what they learn.<br>*[How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Kepler](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-kepler.md)* |

*Why it matters: It decides whether the team's quota-equivalent is renewals and adoption or shipped platform primitives, and whether change management is in scope or handed to system integrators.*

## Practical Guidance

**Do:**

- Test whether you need an FDE motion with the two-axis check: only technical-product/non-technical-buyer justifies it — mature customers with engineers get self-service docs, immature low-customization customers get a standard SaaS deployment.
- Architect every customer-specific solution for the next four customers who will ask for the same thing, because enterprise asks recur with high regularity.
- Staff at least two engineers per customer engagement so a single-person deployment is not a single point of failure, and require a named counterpart working team on the customer side.
- If a customer problem is under a day of work, just build and ship it rather than routing it through PM process — small solved problems are how you earn the right to define product strategy.
- Treat any manual step your engineers perform as a missing product feature and upstream it; Decagon cut over to self-serve after the 25th hand-built integration.
- Reduce every enterprise value claim to increasing revenue, decreasing cost, or mitigating risk, and tie the engagement's success metric to one of the three in writing before the deal closes.
- Open a pitch with the customer's pain — the exact moment they want to throw the laptop out a window — and get wound, fix, and how into roughly 20 seconds; then check that a 17-year-old could restate what you do.
- Replace abstract benefit claims with a concrete before/after: 30 minutes digging through docs versus one question answered in 10 seconds with sources attached.
- Ship every quick fix as if it will run for 18 months, because any hack that solves a real problem becomes permanent and its author becomes its permanent owner.
- Own the enterprise's vocabulary through your platform's ontology — when users adopt your language, other tools build on top of you.
- Tell customers plainly when your product is the wrong tool for a use case; the credibility converts into later opportunities.
- For developer tools, diagnose with product signal versus revenue: signal ahead of revenue means fix distribution, revenue ahead of signal means fix the product.
- Maintain the PR hygiene, tagging, and descriptions that content pipelines depend on — an accurate changelog requires an accurate diff of your product.

**Avoid:**

- Selling a fixed number of engineers for a fixed period with no defined problem — 'take two FDEs for six months, do whatever you want' is a failure mode, not a contract.
- Accepting engagements where the customer's stated reason is that they are understaffed; that is staff augmentation and your engineers will get bored and quit.
- Using the FDE team for product 101/201 sessions, company-wide training workshops, bug filing, or documentation upkeep.
- Building the agent as an accumulating pile of customer-specific prompts and patches — it becomes a black box that is too brittle for the vendor and un-ownable by the customer.
- Taking customer requirements documents at face value; customers describe solutions, not problems, and whoever defines the problem owns the solution.
- Running discovery from a conference room or through surveys instead of being physically on site where the problems are.
- Forcing one canonical schema on an enterprise whose sales, ops, finance, and dev teams each have their own word for the same entity.
- Opening a pitch with category buzzwords like 'agentic AI orchestration platform' or 'agent observability platform'.
- Bolting enterprise requirements — audit, security, evals — onto a successful POC as they surface; that produces brittle systems that do not generalize.
- Publishing AI skills or generated content without regard for their structure — structure, not taste, is what separates polished output from purple-gradient slop.

## Notable Outliers

- Palantir's ~$4M ACV is the highest of any public SaaS company in the Fortune 500 — ahead of ServiceNow at $1.2M and Workday at ~$600K, with no other public SaaS company cracking half a million. ([Forward Deployed Engineering 101](../talks/forward-deployed-engineering-101.md), [6:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KwhgfwOSToQ&t=414s))
- The term 'forward deployed engineer' doesn't exist as a coherent role — it describes so many jobs it means nothing — and hiring managers should instead ask candidates what 'vintage' of FDE they are. ([The Dirty Secret of Forward Deployed Engineering](../talks/the-dirty-secret-of-forward-deployed-engineering.md), [2:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=141s))
- Most software pricing in this market will move to outcome-based, with usage-based pricing (foundation model APIs) as the intermediate stop. ([The Dirty Secret of Forward Deployed Engineering](../talks/the-dirty-secret-of-forward-deployed-engineering.md), [14:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Byv311hdoHE&t=880s))
- Now that AI coding is so good, the scarce engineering skill is restraint — not building the fast one-off solution. ([How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Decagon](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-decagon.md), [7:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wu2hsRfvV0&t=458s))
- A founder's personal brand — not the model, team, or funding — is the durable moat, and it cannot be delegated; staying in SF after YC reportedly doubles both the amount and speed of fundraising. ([GTM Is You](../talks/gtm-is-you.md), [0:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=43s))
- Billboards work as a credibility proxy specifically for bootstrapped companies, which have no funding announcements to signal traction with. ([GTM Is You](../talks/gtm-is-you.md), [5:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=352s))
- FDEs are valuable in early sales not because they are good at talking to customers but because they solve small problems that win trust. ([How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Kepler](../talks/how-forward-deployed-engineering-is-done-at-kepler.md), [7:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OMHGsUZiqA&t=451s))
- Anchor an unfamiliar product category to an already-viral public incident — 'If McDonald's had used us, that drive-thru never would have made it to TikTok.' ([Your AI Product Will Fail Unless You Can Explain It](../talks/your-ai-product-will-fail-unless-you-can-explain-it.md), [2:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Ftrl3vfV0&t=168s))
- 2026 was the year of the creative technologist; 2027 will be the year of the content engineer, with declarative content pipelines generating walkthroughs, docs, and product updates from the codebase. ([Content Is Code](../talks/content-is-code.md), [9:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=574s))
