# Content Is Code

*Program title: Tokenmaxxing is the New "Lines of Code"*

**Speakers:** [Nicholas Arcolano](../speakers/nicholas-arcolano.md)

**Org:** Conductor

**Track:** AI Architects: Tokenmaxxing &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Day/Room:** Day 3 — Session Day 2 &middot; Leadership 2 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Duration:** 10m 53s

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U)

## Summary

Matt Palmer, who leads developer experience at Conductor, argues that technical content — docs, changelogs, product tours, videos, marketing assets — is 'shifting left' into code as the source of truth. He frames three eras: handcrafted content limited by time/money/skill, an era where code existed but engineers were the expensive bottleneck, and today's era where AI makes code generation cheap enough that React/TypeScript becomes a viable medium for non-engineers. His contrarian claim is that the scarce resource is not taste but structure: clean PRs, design tokens, brand guidelines, and accurate internal documentation are what separate polished output from 'AI purple gradient slop.' He reframes this as conscientiousness — meticulousness and care rather than raw technical skill — and predicts 2027 as the year of the 'content engineer' with declarative content pipelines. Worth watching if you own DevRel, docs, or product marketing and want a concrete argument for why your codebase's hygiene now determines your content quality.

## Key Points

- Content — meaning technical communication broadly, including documentation, changelogs, product update emails, marketing, and video — is increasingly produced through code rather than handcrafted in medium-specific tools.
- Palmer describes three eras: handcrafted content constrained by time, money, and skill; an era where code existed but professional engineers were an expensive bottleneck; and the current era where AI makes code cheap.
- In the expensive-code era, content was deprioritized because engineers had higher-value uses of their time, which is why frameworks like Remotion did not proliferate then.
- He demoed a full Conductor product tour rebuilt entirely in React and Remotion, recreating the product surface and walking through a sample user flow — admittedly buggy, but a preview of what becomes normal.
- Despite a Python/data-engineering background, Palmer says his favorite medium is now TypeScript, because React/TypeScript/CSS/HTML is the fastest path to high-fidelity assets.
- The contrarian thesis is that structure, not taste, is the expensive input: consistent brand guidelines, design tokens, separated front-end/back-end code, clean and tagged PRs, and accurate internal docs.
- Accurate changelogs are structurally hard because they require an accurate diff of the product — otherwise someone has to comb through PRs manually.
- Palmer reframes structure as conscientiousness, arguing AI rewards organizational excellence over being the smartest or most technically skilled person in the room.
- He predicts that after 2026, the year of the creative technologist, 2027 will be the year of the content engineer, with declarative content pipelines producing walkthroughs, docs, screenshots, and product updates.

## Positions

- Code is now the fastest way to produce any asset, not just software — including video, websites, slides, and images. ([5:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=315s), confidence: stated)
- Structure, not taste, is the expensive and scarce input in AI-assisted content production. ([6:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=402s), confidence: stated)
- Almost no organizations actually maintain the PR hygiene, tagging, and internal documentation that AI-generated content quality depends on. ([7:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=442s), confidence: stated)
- AI agents cannot solve organizational problems without documentation or skills to draw on, regardless of model quality. ([7:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=442s), confidence: stated)
- Success with AI is increasingly decoupled from being the smartest or most technically skilled person in the room, and instead depends on conscientiousness. ([8:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=483s), confidence: stated)
- Most AI skills currently published are low quality because they were generated without regard for their contents or structure. ([8:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=483s), confidence: stated)
- 2027 will be the year of the 'content engineer,' following 2026 as the year of the creative technologist. ([9:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=574s), confidence: stated)
- Teams with the most disciplined software creation processes will also be the best-communicating teams. ([9:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=574s), confidence: stated)
- Content was historically deprioritized not because it lacked value but because engineer time was too expensive to spend on it. ([4:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=277s), confidence: implied)
- Fully code-generated product tours are not yet production-quality, but will become the norm. ([2:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yv6xovSsB1U&t=161s), confidence: stated)
