# GTM Is You

*Program title: The best SDLC is the one you build yourself: Why orchestration changes everything*

**Speakers:** [Andrei Bocan](../speakers/andrei-bocan.md), [Shane Wolf](../speakers/shane-wolf.md)

**Org:** Evil Martians

**Track:** Workshops Day 1 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Day/Room:** Day 1 — Workshop Day &middot; Track 4 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Duration:** 12m 35s

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA)

## Summary

Victoria Melnikova, who runs new business at the devtool consultancy Evil Martians, argues that in 2026 the scarce resource in developer tools is distribution, not building, and that a founder's personal brand is the most underrated competitive advantage. She frames the problem with Evil Martians' 'product market fit compass' — a two-axis model of product signal vs. revenue — noting that nine out of ten early-stage devtool startups they work with have stronger product signal than revenue, meaning distribution is the gap. She then lays out go-to-market hygiene (crystallize the value proposition, validate with paying customers, find your shelf space in a developer's life, map friends and foes, distribute before the product is ready) and reinforces it with clips from founders at Sentry, Typesense, Supabase, and others on why SF proximity, billboards, events, and public failure all work. The through-line is that AI can amplify a human signal but cannot replace it, so founder-led sales and unapologetic personal quirks are the durable moat. Worth watching if you're a technical founder who hates marketing and wants a concrete, opinionated checklist plus real founder testimony.

## Key Points

- Building software has gotten easy enough that the bottleneck has shifted from product to distribution, which is where most early-stage devtool companies are actually stuck.
- Evil Martians' product market fit compass plots product signal against revenue: stronger signal means fix distribution, stronger revenue means fix the product, and parity means push forward at full speed.
- In nine out of ten early-stage devtool engagements, product signal outpaces revenue — largely because technical founders avoid marketing.
- AI-generated outreach has made inboxes and feeds nearly unreadable, raising the bar for cutting through with something recognizably human.
- The six-step GTM hygiene checklist: crystallize the value proposition, validate with paying customers, identify your shelf space in a developer's day, become the authority on the problem, map allies and competitors, and distribute early — even pre-product.
- Being physically in San Francisco is presented as an unfair but real network advantage, especially for fundraising, per David Cramer of Sentry.
- Billboards function as a credibility proxy — Typesense used hundreds of SF billboards specifically because, without VC funding milestones, they had no other public signal of scale.
- Failure stories attract audiences rather than repelling them, especially with an elegant recovery; one founder walked away from $300K ARR and says he should have done it sooner.
- AI cannot replace a personal brand but can amplify whatever signal you already produce, which makes founder-led sales and craft-oriented work more valuable, not less.

## Positions

- The bottleneck for developer tools startups in 2026 is distribution, not building software. ([1:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=98s), confidence: stated)
- A founder's personal brand is a more durable competitive advantage than the model, team, or funding. ([0:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=43s), confidence: stated)
- Across 37 analyzed devtools, product signal versus revenue is a sufficient diagnostic for whether to invest in distribution or product. ([2:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=142s), confidence: stated)
- Nine out of ten early-stage devtool startups have stronger product signal than revenue, meaning distribution is the constraint. ([2:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=142s), confidence: stated)
- Technical founders' dislike of marketing is the root cause of the distribution gap, not a lack of marketing knowledge. ([2:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=142s), confidence: implied)
- Staying in San Francisco after YC doubles both the amount and speed of fundraising. ([4:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=276s), confidence: stated)
- Physical presence in SF is an unfair but real advantage that founders should take despite online sentiment against it. ([5:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=322s), confidence: stated)
- Billboards work as a credibility proxy for bootstrapped companies that lack funding announcements to signal traction. ([5:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=352s), confidence: stated)
- Companies should distribute and market before the product is finished. ([3:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=234s), confidence: stated)
- Publicly sharing failures attracts audiences to a brand more effectively than projecting perfection. ([8:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=531s), confidence: stated)
- AI can amplify a person's output but cannot substitute for the person, so personal brands will remain defensible through 2030. ([11:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=704s), confidence: stated)
- Audience preference is swinging back toward craft and artisanal work as a reaction to AI automation. ([10:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=614s), confidence: stated)
- Personal brand building cannot be delegated by a founder. ([0:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G6IlDzj8OjA&t=43s), confidence: stated)
