# Forward Deployed Engineering at Cursor

*Program title: How Forward Deployed Engineering is done at Cursor*

**Speakers:** [Pauline Brunet](../speakers/pauline-brunet.md)

**Org:** Cursor

**Track:** Forward Deployed Engineering &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Day/Room:** Day 2 — Session Day 1 &middot; Track 8 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Duration:** 20m 47s

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4)

## Summary

Pauline Brunet, who leads Cursor's global forward deployed engineering (FDE) team, lays out an operating playbook for building an FDE function from scratch. Her core framework is a 2x2 matrix of customer digital maturity against product customizability: FDE only earns its cost in the middle band, and the corners are better served by self-service docs, traditional SaaS deployment, or advisory work. She is emphatic that FDE is not staff augmentation and not professional services — requests framed as 'we're understaffed' are a red flag, and a scope of 'take two FDEs for six months' is a recipe for failure. The rest of the talk is concrete operational advice: hire 5+ year engineers with customer-facing chops, define success and baselines up front, keep scope directional rather than fixed, always co-develop with a named customer team, and reduce every ROI story to increasing revenue, decreasing costs, or mitigating risk. Worth watching if you are standing up or scoping a deployed-engineering org; less so if you want technical detail on Cursor's products.

## Key Points

- Before building an FDE function, map your target customers' digital maturity against how customizable your product is; FDE fits a middle band, while the mature/low-customization and low-maturity/low-customization corners are better served by self-service documentation or a traditional SaaS deployment.
- FDE is explicitly not staff augmentation — when a customer says they need help because they are understaffed, Brunet treats it as a red flag and screens for it by asking who on the customer side will form the working team.
- Cursor's FDE projects are scoped with the economic buyer or a senior champion so the work maps to a strategic objective with real ROI, ensuring the deployed agents and automations are not turned off after the team leaves.
- Cursor hires 'unicorns' for the founding team — engineers with 5+ years of experience and customer-facing ability, no early-career hires yet — with a plan to split the role into more-technical and more-customer-facing tracks as the team grows.
- The team is organized by geography today but expects to move to industry alignment, because failing to speak an industry's lingo (payments, asset management, risk for a bank) immediately costs credibility.
- Scope should be directional rather than a fixed deliverable list, because the FDE team hasn't seen the customer's data, systems, or processes up front and both sides will want to pivot as they learn.
- Every engagement should define success with a measurable baseline at the start (e.g. a three-hour process reduced to 20 minutes) and then actually measure against it at the end.
- All ROI reduces to three things — increasing revenue, decreasing costs, or mitigating risk — and FDEs should over-communicate which one they're driving, since customers often fail to frame agent spend against the cost it displaces.
- The FDE team functions as the tip of the spear for edge-case use cases beyond the software development lifecycle (HR, finance, supply chain, e-commerce) and as the tightest product feedback loop the company has.
- Partner with system integrators and consultancies to cover work the FDE team shouldn't do — change management, broad rollouts of mature products — and to extend reach at scale.

## Positions

- FDE is a poor investment for customers at either low-customization corner of the maturity matrix — mature customers should get self-service documentation, immature ones a traditional SaaS deployment. ([2:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=156s), confidence: stated)
- Running product training workshops or 101/201 sessions is not a good use of the FDE function. ([4:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=273s), confidence: stated)
- A customer saying they need FDE help because they are understaffed is a red flag indicating staff augmentation rather than a real FDE project. ([9:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=575s), confidence: stated)
- A successful FDE engagement is one where the customer keeps the deployed systems running after the team leaves. ([8:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=500s), confidence: stated)
- Founding FDE teams should hire engineers with 5+ years of experience and customer-facing ability rather than early-career hires; role splitting comes later as the team scales. ([11:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=679s), confidence: stated)
- Geography-based FDE org structures should eventually give way to industry-based ones because domain vocabulary determines credibility with buyers. ([11:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=718s), confidence: stated)
- Selling a fixed allocation of FDEs for a fixed period without a defined problem is a recipe for failure; scope should be problem-anchored and directional. ([16:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=995s), confidence: stated)
- Running a customer engagement without a named counterpart working team on the customer side is a structural failure of the engagement. ([18:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=1101s), confidence: stated)
- Telling a customer that your product is the wrong tool for their use case increases trust and generates future opportunities. ([14:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=889s), confidence: stated)
- All enterprise value from AI deployments reduces to increasing revenue, decreasing costs, or mitigating risk. ([19:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=1172s), confidence: stated)
- Change management and broad product rollouts are better delegated to system integrator and consulting partners than done by the FDE team. ([14:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=857s), confidence: stated)
- Launching a project quickly and risking failure teaches more than extended planning. ([13:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=824s), confidence: stated)
- The FDE team is the company's earliest and highest-signal source of input on what to build next. ([6:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APqXGyCoGW4&t=392s), confidence: stated)
