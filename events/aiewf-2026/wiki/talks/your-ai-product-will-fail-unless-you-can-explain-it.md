# Your AI Product Will Fail Unless You Can Explain It

**Speakers:** [Veronica Hylak](../speakers/veronica-hylak.md)

**Org:** Hey AI

**Duration:** 6m 01s

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Ftrl3vfV0)

## Summary

Veronica Hylak argues that most AI products fail not because the technology is weak but because nobody can explain them, and offers a three-part storytelling method for fixing that. Step one is to open with 'the wound' — the customer's concrete daily pain — rather than with what you built. Step two is to make the product click by anchoring it to an image or viral story a 17-year-old would instantly grasp, deferring precise technical definitions until later in the sale. Step three is to show a before/after transformation with specifics instead of vague claims like 'we improve productivity.' It's a fast, practical six-minute talk aimed at founders and product marketers who keep losing rooms to jargon-heavy pitches.

## Key Points

- Generic AI pitches built from stacked buzzwords ('agentic orchestration platform for enterprise knowledge retrieval') make listeners feel nothing and are a major reason AI products stall in the market.
- The first slide should immerse the audience in their own workday and the thing they are already tired of doing, never in what the company built.
- The wound-to-fix sequence should take about 20 seconds: identify the wound, say 'we fix that,' then show how.
- The comprehension bar for a pitch is whether a 17-year-old could understand what you do; failing that test loses the room.
- Tying a product to an already-viral story (the McDonald's AI drive-thru putting bacon on ice cream) borrows existing public understanding instead of building it from scratch.
- Concrete mental images like 'Devin, the AI software engineer' or 'a smoke alarm for AI behavior' work as front doors even though they aren't precise technical definitions — the technical version comes later, to close.
- Abstract value claims ('we improve code quality with AI') should be replaced with a specific before/after: 30 minutes digging through docs and tickets becomes one question answered in 10 seconds with sources attached.
- Distribution has changed: building something great is no longer enough for the market to find you, and the clearest stories are what get funded, bought, and talked about.

## Positions

- Many AI products are failing in the market primarily because their pitches are incomprehensible, not because the technology is inadequate. ([0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Ftrl3vfV0&t=0s), confidence: stated)
- A pitch should open with the customer's pain rather than with a description of what the company built. ([0:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Ftrl3vfV0&t=43s), confidence: stated)
- The wound, the fix, and the how can and should be delivered in roughly 20 seconds. ([2:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Ftrl3vfV0&t=122s), confidence: stated)
- If a 17-year-old cannot understand what your product does, you will lose the room. ([2:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Ftrl3vfV0&t=122s), confidence: stated)
- Imprecise but vivid descriptions are acceptable — even preferable — as an opening, because technical precision can be supplied later in the sales conversation. ([3:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Ftrl3vfV0&t=212s), confidence: stated)
- Buzzword-laden category language like 'agentic AI orchestration platform' or 'agent observability platform' actively harms comprehension and should not be used to open. ([2:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Ftrl3vfV0&t=168s), confidence: stated)
- Abstract benefit statements such as 'we increase productivity' fail to convey value; only a concrete before/after comparison does. ([4:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Ftrl3vfV0&t=252s), confidence: stated)
- Product quality alone no longer generates market discovery the way it did roughly 15 years ago; clarity of story now determines which products get funded and bought. ([4:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Ftrl3vfV0&t=292s), confidence: stated)
- Anchoring a pitch to an already-viral public incident is one of the most efficient ways to make an unfamiliar product category legible. ([2:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Ftrl3vfV0&t=168s), confidence: implied)
