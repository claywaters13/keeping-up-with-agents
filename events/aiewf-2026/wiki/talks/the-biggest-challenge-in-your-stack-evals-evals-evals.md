# "The biggest challenge in your stack? Evals, Evals, Evals"

**Speakers:** 2026 State of AI Engineering results *(not resolved to a speaker profile)*

**Org:** Amplify

**Track:** AI Architects &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Duration:** 19m 47s

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI)

## Summary

Barr, an investment partner at Amplify, presents results from the 2026 State of AI Engineering survey (1,048 respondents, run in partnership with Notion and Vercel). The data-heavy talk tracks year-over-year shifts: image generation adoption doubled, audio has the highest intent-to-adopt for the second year running, cost has become a first-class engineering constraint that three in four respondents actively manage, and open-weight models augment rather than replace closed models. The headline finding is on agents: 95% of respondents now use them and 89% of those agents have write access, tripling the share of write-enabled agents year over year — while control mechanisms remain primitive human-in-the-loop approvals and permission gating. Evals remain the number one stack challenge for the third consecutive year, though by a shrinking margin, and 96% of respondents report a problem somewhere in their stack. Worth watching if you want a quantitative snapshot of what AI engineers are actually building with, buying versus building, and betting on.

## Key Points

- The survey drew 1,048 respondents and shows AI engineering is a discipline rather than a job title, spanning founders, CTOs, product people, and engineers across company sizes.
- For the third year running, respondents skew senior in software but new to AI: over half of those with 10+ years of software experience have three years or less of AI experience.
- Image generation saw the biggest year-over-year jump, with respondents building on it doubling from 18% to 36%, while audio has the strongest intent to adopt at 56% (up from 37% last year).
- 94% use closed models and 45% use open-weight models, but over 90% of open-weight users also use closed models — open weights augment rather than replace.
- Open versus closed was a top-three model selection criterion for only 5% of respondents; quality dominates, followed by agentic capabilities and cost, while only one in five named reliability.
- Cost has become a first-class engineering constraint: 40% say it regularly shapes how ambitiously they use AI and another 36% say it sometimes does, and cost/token usage is the number two thing monitored in production after quality.
- Agent usage roughly doubled to 95%, and write access among agent builders jumped from 52% to 89%, tripling the overall share of write-enabled agents — yet control is still mostly human-in-the-loop approvals and permission gating.
- Evals are the number one stack challenge every year the survey has run, but the margin is narrowing and 96% report some stack problem; vibe review remains the most common evaluation method.
- Build-vs-buy splits cleanly: inference and model serving is the layer people buy most, while 61% build prompt management themselves and product-logic layers (prompts, RAG, eval) stay in-house.
- 97% report a net positive organizational effect — chiefly cheaper failure and more experimentation — but over 9 in 10 also report negative downstream effects like erosion of deep technical skills and codebase understanding, and 81% say AI is blurring role boundaries.

## Positions

- Open-weight models are augmenting rather than replacing closed models, since over 90% of open-weight users also run closed models. ([6:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=368s), confidence: stated)
- The open-vs-closed debate gets disproportionate airtime relative to its influence on actual model choice, which is driven by quality, agentic capability, and cost. ([6:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=368s), confidence: stated)
- Reliability has become a threshold requirement rather than a differentiator, which is why only one in five respondents named it a top-three consideration. ([6:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=413s), confidence: stated)
- Standardization is happening at the platform and tooling layer, not at the model layer — 87% of teams use more than one model while over half say their orgs are standardizing on fewer AI tools. ([7:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=456s), confidence: stated)
- Cost is now a first-class engineering constraint, monitored in production like an SLA and second only to quality. ([8:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=496s), confidence: stated)
- Agent guardrails have not kept pace with agent capability; no one has settled the control layer, and current tooling amounts to intern management. ([10:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=652s), confidence: stated)
- Agent failures are predominantly reasoning failures — hallucination and losing context mid-task frustrate about two-thirds of respondents — rather than infrastructure failures. ([11:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=695s), confidence: stated)
- Evals are the number one stack challenge for the third year running, but the lead margin is shrinking as pain points fragment across the stack. ([12:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=732s), confidence: stated)
- Layers closer to product logic (prompts, RAG, eval) stay in-house while inference and model serving is the clear buy market. ([13:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=811s), confidence: stated)
- Fine-tuning is a 'not yet' layer — most teams do not do it at all, and those who chose build or buy are not switching. ([13:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=811s), confidence: stated)
- Cheap code generation carries a real cost in eroded deep technical skills and codebase understanding, reported by over nine in ten respondents as some form of negative downstream effect. ([14:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=858s), confidence: stated)
- Shipping software is no longer gated on being an engineer, with over a third of teams having non-developers ship features and 17% shipping customer-facing ones. ([16:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=967s), confidence: stated)
- An AGI declaration by a leading lab within five years is expected by 67% of respondents, but the question measured a press release rather than a technical achievement. ([16:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=1013s), confidence: stated)
- Practitioners are broadly skeptical that Transformers will remain state-of-the-art in five years, with only 9% betting on it. ([17:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RGe6EjucbzI&t=1057s), confidence: stated)
