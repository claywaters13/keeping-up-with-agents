# Computer Use at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice

**Speakers:** [Pierluca D'Oro](../speakers/pierluca-d-oro.md)

**Org:** Programma Labs

**Track:** Computer Use &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Day/Room:** Day 3 — Session Day 2 &middot; Track 7 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Duration:** 17m 28s

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY)

## Summary

Pierluca D'Oro argues that most computer-use agent benchmarks are quietly broken in two ways: their environments are deterministic enough to be gamed, and their metrics understate uncertainty. He opens with a striking demonstration — a 'replay agent' that blindly replays recorded successful action sequences matches or beats the frontier model it was distilled from on benchmarks like OSWorld and Mobile World, and he shows formally that pass@k on deterministic environments is equivalent to evaluating that replay agent. The fix he proposes has two halves: the PRISM principles for environment design (multifactorial variation, verified configurations, sandboxing, realism, privileged-information verifiers), realized in a mobile benchmark called DigWorld with 15 Android apps, 387 verified scenarios, and 3.2 million verified configurations; and hierarchical confidence intervals that account for both action stochasticity and environment variation. The uncertainty point has teeth: naive rollout-only confidence intervals achieve only ~17-20% coverage instead of the nominal 95%, which can produce confidently wrong model-deployment decisions costing hundreds of thousands of dollars a month at scale. Worth watching if you build or trust agent evals and want a concrete, quantified account of how they mislead you.

## Key Points

- A 'replay agent' — a sub-megabyte script that blindly replays recorded successful action sequences per task — matches or exceeds the frontier model it was extracted from on standard computer-use benchmarks, because those benchmarks are static and deterministic.
- The paper proves formally that pass@k on a deterministic environment is literally equivalent to measuring the success rate of the replay agent, so the metric encodes the exploit rather than resisting it.
- The PRISM principles prescribe environments that are multifactorial (varying data, appearance, and initial state), verified for combination validity, sandboxed, realistic, and equipped with privileged-information verifiers; no existing benchmark satisfies all of them.
- DigWorld instantiates these principles as 15 Android apps across domains, 387 verified scenarios, and 3.2 million verified configurations generated by varying instance values, data profiles, themes, and starting screens.
- Coding agents can mass-produce environment software, but volume is not the bottleneck — the hard part is a compiler-like verification pipeline that generates all configs and rejects the invalid ones, leaving diversity that is also correct.
- On a properly varied benchmark the replay agent's performance collapses to near zero, which is the diagnostic signal that a benchmark is not replay-hackable.
- Frontier models turn out to be surprisingly non-robust across variation axes — changing only the starting screen or the app theme materially degrades performance on tasks the model otherwise handles.
- Evaluation must capture two distinct sources of stochasticity — action variation across rollouts and environment variation across configurations — and ignoring the second yields ~17-20% empirical coverage from nominal 95% confidence intervals.
- Overconfident intervals cause real financial harm: a 4% true performance gap across a million tasks at roughly $12-20 per mistake runs to hundreds of thousands of dollars per month, whereas honest intervals would have said 'not confident enough to decide.'

## Positions

- A replay agent that blindly replays recorded action sequences achieves the same or better success rate than the frontier model it was extracted from on standard benchmarks like OSWorld and Mobile World. ([0:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=59s), confidence: stated)
- Pass@k evaluated on a deterministic environment is formally equivalent to measuring the success rate of a replay agent, making it an inappropriate metric for computer-use tasks. ([1:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=108s), confidence: stated)
- Static, deterministic benchmarks are inherently gameable and should be replaced by multifactorial environments that vary data, appearance, and initial state. ([1:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=108s), confidence: stated)
- No existing computer-use benchmark satisfies all five PRISM environment-design principles simultaneously. ([5:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=346s), confidence: stated)
- DigWorld comprises 15 Android apps, 387 verified scenarios, and 3.2 million verified configurations. ([5:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=346s), confidence: stated)
- The bottleneck in building diverse environments is not generating software with coding agents but verifying that generated configurations are valid. ([7:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=427s), confidence: stated)
- Most frontier models are not robust to variations in starting screen or app theme, despite performing well on the base version of the same task. ([9:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=595s), confidence: stated)
- Confidence intervals computed from rollouts alone achieve only about 17-20% empirical coverage instead of the nominal 95%. ([12:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=746s), confidence: stated)
- Accounting for the hierarchical structure of the benchmark when computing confidence intervals restores coverage to the nominal 90-95%. ([13:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=788s), confidence: stated)
- Overconfident confidence intervals can cause model-deployment mistakes costing hundreds of thousands of dollars per month at a scale of one million tasks with a 4% true performance gap. ([13:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=829s), confidence: stated)
- Varying the initial state across runs is rare in existing benchmarks but should be standard practice in evals. ([15:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=913s), confidence: stated)
- Continuing to use a known-gameable benchmark harms the field by directing optimization toward scores that do not capture what practitioners care about. ([15:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=959s), confidence: stated)
- A benchmark is adequately de-gamed only if a replay agent extracted from it scores near zero on it. ([9:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CTLa_p6iOiY&t=556s), confidence: implied)
