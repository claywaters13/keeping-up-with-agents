# Designing Icons & Graphics For the Original Mac

**Speakers:** [Susan Kare](../speakers/susan-kare.md)

**Duration:** 51m 44s

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw)

## Summary

Susan Kare, the artist who designed the original Macintosh's icons and system fonts, walks through how the Mac's visual language was made under brutal constraints (16x16 and 32x32 black-and-white bitmaps, 128K of memory) and what that taught her about design that lasts. Her core argument is that icons succeed on idea and metaphor rather than fidelity: less detail makes an image more universal, and depicting a specific product (the ImageWriter's sprocket paper, the 3.5-inch floppy) dates it. She traces the same principle through later work for Microsoft, General Magic, Facebook, and Pinterest, including hard commercial lessons — on Facebook Gifts, 'cute trumped everything' and expensive-looking luxury drawings lost to teddy bears and penguins. The last third is a collected-advice reel from Paul Rand, Steve Jobs, Alan Kay, Andy Hertzfeld, and her father, covering logo strategy, why design contests produce bad creative, showing decision-makers several options instead of one, and the fact that nothing ever takes an hour. Worth watching for founders and designers who want a concrete account of shipping a product identity fast with almost no tooling, plus first-hand startup lore from the Mac group.

## Key Points

- The Macintosh group's explicit brief was a computer anyone could use, understandable without a manual and friendly enough to be welcoming, and that goal drove every graphic decision.
- Kare argues constraints are creatively productive as long as you understand and embrace them rather than resent what you lack — 16x16 black-and-white pixels forced clarity.
- Drawing on Scott McCloud's Understanding Comics, she holds that detail makes an image specific and less detail makes it universal: a simple pencil communicates 'writing' better than a photorealistic chrome pen.
- Icons should avoid depicting particular product designs; the print icon with ImageWriter sprocket holes and the floppy-disk save icon aged badly, while metaphors survive.
- Abstract symbols like the command-key cloverleaf are harder to remember than symbols whose metaphor reinforces the concept, even when they are easy to draw.
- Andy Hertzfeld's crude icon editor — no shapes, no undo, just toggling bits and auto-generated hex — was transformative because it enabled fast iteration; Kare says a thousand dots plus enough iteration yields real resemblance.
- Commercial lesson from four years of Facebook Gifts: cute beat expensive-looking, the kiss mark was the all-time best seller, and a hit was predictable from sales in the first 15 minutes after midnight launch.
- Company-building advice she endorses: consensus hiring with everyone in one room works up to roughly 15 people and breaks down after that.
- Paul Rand's practices she adopted — use few fonts well, tie the mark to the product's name rather than an arbitrary symbol, and refuse spec work (he charged NeXT $100,000 for one logo instead of joining a five-way contest).
- When presenting to a strong-opinioned decision-maker like Steve Jobs, show several options so they can reject one and still choose, rather than a single proposal.

## Positions

- Constraints improve creative work as long as you understand them and treat them as a starting point rather than a deprivation. ([7:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=443s), confidence: stated)
- Less detail makes an image more universal; highly detailed faces and objects read as someone or something specific and block projection. ([16:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=991s), confidence: stated)
- Icons should not depict a particular product design, because the product dates and the icon dates with it; a metaphor lasts longer. ([21:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=1260s), confidence: stated)
- Abstract symbols are harder for users to remember than symbols whose metaphor reinforces the underlying concept. ([22:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=1373s), confidence: stated)
- The value of the Mac's icon editor came from enabling rapid iteration, not from features — it had no shapes, no undo, and no eraser. ([13:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=813s), confidence: implied)
- Avoiding text in interface elements like the control panel let the product ship internationally without producing a different version. ([12:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=745s), confidence: stated)
- Design contests with multiple paid finalists are not a good way to get good creative work. ([38:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=2308s), confidence: stated)
- A logo should do something with the product's name rather than attach an arbitrary symbol, because most companies cannot afford to buy meaning for a random mark. ([40:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=2412s), confidence: stated)
- You need only a few typefaces; skill in using them matters more than quantity. ([37:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=2260s), confidence: stated)
- Present several options to a powerful reviewer rather than one, so they can participate by rejecting some and still select a direction. ([49:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=2948s), confidence: stated)
- Do not play with or vary a logo until the audience knows it well enough that variation reads as play. ([49:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=2948s), confidence: stated)
- Consensus hiring with everyone in one room stays workable up to about 15 people and degrades beyond that, with both upsides and downsides. ([47:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=2879s), confidence: stated)
- Creative projects consistently take longer than estimated — nothing takes an hour — so schedule extra time. ([41:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=2479s), confidence: stated)
- In consumer digital goods, cuteness outsells perceived luxury: teddy bears, hearts, and penguins beat diamond earrings and a Rolex at the same price. ([32:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=1977s), confidence: stated)
- Telling users a product is easy backfires when they struggle, so it is better to let them discover it and feel smart. ([46:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEvLKzsEwMw&t=2811s), confidence: implied)
