# Average Is Not Good Enough

**Speakers:** [Max Hodak](../speakers/max-hodak.md)

**Org:** Science

**Duration:** 57m 02s

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8)

## Summary

Max Hodak, CEO of Science (retinal prosthesis, ex-Neuralink), argues that what separates successful deep tech startups from failures is almost never the technology — it's the boring operational infrastructure that determines how fast the company can iterate. He walks through concrete systems his company built in-house: a procurement and budgeting layer that attributes real costs to experiments (a wafer iteration costs $40,000), a four-stage hiring funnel whose first screen is company-wide voting, and "eigen reviews," a PageRank-like continuous performance review that replaces the annual 360 cycle. The through-line is that speed compounds, speed comes from infrastructure, and infrastructure is now buildable rather than purchasable because agents can vibe-code internal tools. The long Q&A covers PhD-vs-industry, AI-resistant take-homes, raising 2x the cost of your next value inflection, pushing for profitability earlier, and why he sees BCI as a longevity story before an AI one. Worth watching if you run a hardware, bio, or otherwise physical-world company and want specific operating mechanics rather than startup aphorisms.

## Key Points

- Approving individual purchases is the wrong place to control burn — spending review has to happen upstream at the budgeting stage so employees can make tradeoffs inside a resource envelope they understand.
- Bulk purchasing destroys cost attribution, and when nobody knows what an experiment costs, experiments feel free; Science built internal software (Helix) that links purchasing through manufacturing so every loop has a dollar figure.
- A $20M Series A supporting 20 people and 20,000 square feet leaves roughly $3M/year of actual research budget, which goes far faster than founders expect.
- Science's hiring funnel has exactly four stages — company-wide applicant voting, a company-wide phone screen for judgment/horsepower/agency, a take-home, and a full interview — with 17% of applicants reaching a phone screen and a target of at least 25% interview-to-offer conversion.
- Take-homes should be AI-resistant by design — high-ceiling, non-saturating, and scorable to two or three numbers you can plot — rather than by banning AI use.
- Annual 360 reviews mostly re-surface problems you already knew about; the alternative is asking employees every four to six weeks whether they'd vote again for a colleague's hire, then weighting votes by an eigenvector-centrality-style graph, with Monte Carlo dropout to detect voting cliques.
- Rate of iteration compounds so severely that an approach with a shorter loop usually beats a technically superior approach with a longer one.
- Deep tech companies rarely die because the technology fails; they die because the founders never build the organizational systems to connect strategy to execution at hundreds of people.
- Founders cannot delegate judgment: at some point years in, with hundreds of millions at stake, there is nobody left to ask, and the decision has to make sense to you alone.
- Building internal tools instead of buying commercial ERP/ATS was historically irrational because software was expensive; agents changed that math, though it still reads as contrarian to a board.
- On fundraising: price out the experiments needed to reach your next value inflection point, raise twice that, and push for profitability sooner than feels necessary so you're valued on your roadmap rather than your probability of dying.

## Positions

- Controlling burn at the point of individual purchase approval is the wrong mechanism; spending review must happen earlier, at the budgeting stage. ([4:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=270s), confidence: stated)
- Speed determines startup success or failure, and speed is determined by operational infrastructure rather than by team intelligence. ([19:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=1164s), confidence: stated)
- Deep tech companies usually fail because they cannot organize human organizations, not because the technology doesn't work. ([25:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=1543s), confidence: stated)
- The four-stage funnel (company-wide vote, phone screen, homework, full interview) is the minimum set of steps needed to make a hiring decision — there is no more efficient process. ([15:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=908s), confidence: stated)
- Interview-to-offer conversion should be at least 25%, or on-sites waste too much of the team's time. ([14:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=874s), confidence: stated)
- Take-homes should be made AI-resistant through non-saturating, high-ceiling, numerically scorable tasks; candidates' use of AI models is otherwise irrelevant. ([13:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=791s), confidence: stated)
- Annual 360 performance reviews are disruptive and mostly resurface known problems; continuous peer voting weighted by eigenvector centrality is more or less the right way to do performance reviews. ([17:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=1058s), confidence: stated)
- When two approaches are otherwise comparable, choose the one with the shorter iteration cycle because compounding dominates other advantages. ([18:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=1124s), confidence: stated)
- A CEO cannot delegate judgment and must make decisions that make sense to them regardless of the momentum behind alternatives. ([20:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=1238s), confidence: stated)
- For most fields, working at a high-performing company adjacent to your target area is better preparation than getting a PhD, because startup culture is an oral tradition you learn by doing. ([26:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=1582s), confidence: stated)
- The best evidence of exceptional ability is winning legible competitive games; it is hard to know you're exceptional without one. ([27:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=1622s), confidence: stated)
- A small team that can hold more of an interdisciplinary problem in their heads outperforms academia's model of deep verticalized experts meeting at an interdisciplinary center, because the latter ships the interfaces between departments. ([33:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=2005s), confidence: stated)
- AI agents have so far been a multiplier for the team rather than a replacement, with the biggest impact in coding and regulatory/quality-system work. ([35:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=2145s), confidence: stated)
- AI and regulation are a better fit than people think — most regulations are written in blood and largely good ideas, and the real bottleneck is humans being bad at reading and interpreting standards. ([36:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=2219s), confidence: stated)
- Companies should build internal software rather than buy it; commercial ERP and ATS products force bottlenecks and nobody loves them, and agents have made building viable. ([38:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=2290s), confidence: stated)
- Founders should price out the experiments needed to reach the next value inflection point and raise twice that amount; keeping waste to 20-30% is a good outcome. ([44:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=2655s), confidence: stated)
- Founders should push for profitability sooner than they think they need to, even in long-horizon deep tech. ([44:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=2695s), confidence: stated)
- In the near term BCI is a longevity and healthcare story rather than an AI story, because neural engineering delivers effect sizes medicine otherwise can't reach. ([40:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=2402s), confidence: stated)
- Consciousness is a practical problem, not a philosophical one — a BCI that could read and drive every neuron would let us figure it out quickly. ([32:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=1924s), confidence: stated)
- Biotech remains capital intensive and he would not recommend it to someone with other options, though the patient impact when it works is unlike any other sector. ([53:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=3217s), confidence: stated)
- A company needs both mission zealots and pragmatic professionals — roughly 20/80 at SpaceX, 30/70 at Science — to succeed over the long term. ([52:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=3127s), confidence: stated)
- Roughly half of a deep tech startup's burn is headcount, leaving about $3M/year of research budget on a $20M Series A. ([8:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xc4klGbq8v8&t=488s), confidence: stated)
