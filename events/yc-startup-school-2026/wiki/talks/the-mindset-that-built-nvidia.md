# The Mindset That Built NVIDIA

**Speakers:** [Jensen Huang](../speakers/jensen-huang.md)

**Org:** NVIDIA

**Duration:** 49m 00s

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ)

## Summary

A fireside with NVIDIA founder-CEO Jensen Huang at YC Startup School 2026, framed around what the early NVIDIA story teaches founders. Huang's central claim is that NVIDIA's founding technology and algorithm were flat wrong, that the company survived only by confronting that in 1995 and re-learning 3D graphics from textbooks bought at Fry's, and that a Sega executive's decision to pay out a $12M contract NVIDIA couldn't fulfill kept it alive. The durable idea, he argues, was never the chip but the belief that accelerating algorithm domains — graphics, molecular dynamics, and eventually deep learning — could solve otherwise intractable problems. He then applies that lens forward: agents are the new software, controllability rather than raw accuracy is the key missing breakthrough, systems thinking is the skill that survives automation, and the 'AI destroys jobs' narrative is backwards (he cites software engineering jobs up 10% and radiology jobs up 20%). Worth watching for founders wanting concrete numbers on physical AI's timeline (a ~$10B business today, next $100B business in under 10 years) and a blunt account of founder psychology: 'how hard can it be?' as an operating attitude.

## Key Points

- NVIDIA's founding technology and algorithm for PC 3D graphics were wrong, and the company only survived because Huang forced the team to confront it in 1995 and relearn graphics from OpenGL textbooks.
- A $12M Sega contract NVIDIA could not fulfill was partially paid out anyway ($5M) after Huang told the CEO the truth — a lesson that investors and partners back people, not companies.
- The one founding idea that held up was that CPUs could be augmented to solve otherwise intractable problems; the business is accelerating algorithm domains, not building great chips.
- Huang read AlexNet as the discovery of a universal function approximator — a new way of doing software — and immediately began reinventing the whole five-layer stack from processor to application.
- He argues founders should shape the company to themselves like an F1 driver fitting a car, and that founder mode scaled at NVIDIA for 34 years; the next CEO can reshape it later.
- The biggest missing capability in agents is fine-grained controllability — changing one word in a plan file and having everything else regenerate — not getting to 100% accuracy.
- Huang claims automation eliminates tasks, not jobs, citing software engineering jobs up 10% year over year and radiology jobs up ~20% despite AI taking over the field, because backlogs of work are enormous.
- Physical AI is already a nearly $10B business for NVIDIA across robotics and autonomous vehicles, and he expects it to become the next $100B business in under 10 years, with the robotics 'ChatGPT moment' already a couple of years behind us.
- Systems thinking is the skill he says will matter most, because low-level implementation will be done agentically and the leverage moves to orchestrating millions of agents.

## Positions

- NVIDIA's founding 3D graphics algorithm was fundamentally wrong, and the company survived only by admitting it in 1995 and relearning graphics from textbooks. ([1:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=77s), confidence: stated)
- What makes great companies is a unique, deeply held perspective about the world, not the technology or the market. ([6:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=363s), confidence: stated)
- NVIDIA's business is accelerating algorithm domains, not building great chips; the chip is downstream of the algorithm. ([6:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=411s), confidence: stated)
- Deep learning's significance was that it is a universal function approximator and therefore a new way of doing software, implying the whole computing stack gets reinvented. ([11:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=666s), confidence: stated)
- Founders should reshape the organization to fit themselves rather than adopt conventional management structures; the next CEO can reshape it again. ([18:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1100s), confidence: stated)
- Founder mode scales — it worked at NVIDIA for 34 years and from zero to trillions in value. ([18:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1136s), confidence: stated)
- Agents do not need to be 100% accurate to be useful; 80% plus human completion is already valuable. ([22:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1348s), confidence: stated)
- Fine-grained controllability is the single biggest breakthrough agents still need, ahead of accuracy or raw capability. ([23:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1389s), confidence: stated)
- Coarse-grained recursive self-improvement already exists in today's agents via markdown files, long-term memory compaction, and knowledge graphs. ([21:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1300s), confidence: stated)
- Hardware companies must plan 5 to 10 years ahead because a system takes ~3 years to build, ~2 to ramp, and should serve 10 years of use. ([24:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1462s), confidence: stated)
- Letting employees choose their own AI coding tools rather than standardizing is the better policy during rapid change. ([24:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1497s), confidence: stated)
- Reasoning from prior knowledge drastically reduces the driving data needed; a couple million miles suffices for a very good self-driving car. ([26:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1573s), confidence: stated)
- Every company should be able to build its own domain-specific AI, and open source tooling now makes that relatively easy. ([28:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1731s), confidence: stated)
- AI eliminates tasks but not jobs, and the narrative that AI destroys jobs is exactly backwards. ([31:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1879s), confidence: stated)
- Software engineering jobs have grown 10% year over year and radiology jobs about 20% in recent years despite automation of their core tasks. ([31:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1917s), confidence: stated)
- Employment grows because backlogs of ideas, patients, and lawsuits are so large that productivity gains expand demand for labor. ([32:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=1971s), confidence: stated)
- The ChatGPT moment for robotics already happened a couple of years ago, in the imagination-opening sense rather than the productivity sense. ([35:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=2137s), confidence: stated)
- Robotics progress now depends on the same eval infrastructure as agents: real-to-sim environments, grounded physics simulation, and sim-to-real reinforcement learning. ([36:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=2173s), confidence: stated)
- Self-driving cars were the right first robotics market because of market size, standardized technology, and real economic value. ([37:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=2244s), confidence: stated)
- NVIDIA open-sourced its self-driving stack because adjacent autonomy markets like agriculture, mail delivery, and warehouse AMRs are individually too small to justify separate stacks. ([38:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=2285s), confidence: stated)
- Physical AI is already a nearly $10 billion business for NVIDIA and will be its next $100 billion business in less than 10 years. ([38:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=2324s), confidence: stated)
- Modern AI would not exist without open source — Linux, Kubernetes, Torch, Theano, TensorFlow, and PyTorch were prerequisites. ([39:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=2396s), confidence: stated)
- Writing code by hand will be automated away like long division, while hard sciences and systems thinking retain their value. ([41:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=2476s), confidence: stated)
- This is the greatest time in 60 years to start a company because the computer itself has been completely reset. ([45:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=2729s), confidence: stated)
- Resilience and belief in one's ability to learn matter more to founders than knowing the answers up front. ([47:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4B37S1dyQQ&t=2844s), confidence: stated)
